Atmospheric River Analysis in EAMxx

Atmospheric rivers (ARs) are long, narrow corridors of strong horizontal water-vapor transport, usually embedded in the warm sector of extratropical cyclones. They are commonly identified from integrated vapor transport (IVT), often with a practical threshold near 250 kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹ together with geometric constraints (length of order 2000 km and a large length-to-width ratio). Although they occupy only a small fraction of the globe at any time, they account for a large share of poleward moisture transport and of extreme precipitation where they make landfall, especially along west coasts.

They matter for both the global water cycle and regional hydroclimate. ARs supply a substantial fraction of cool-season precipitation and snowpack in places such as western North America, western Europe, and parts of South America, while the strongest events drive flooding, landslides, and rain-on-snow runoff. Their intensity is tightly linked to column moisture and low-level winds, so they are sensitive both to thermodynamic moistening and to changes in storm tracks and jet structure.

EAMxx is the atmosphere component used for E3SM climate integrations, including high-resolution and exascale configurations. Whether the model produces realistic AR frequency, intensity, width, landfall, and associated precipitation is a direct test of its moisture transport, extratropical cyclone dynamics, boundary-layer winds, and orographic precipitation. Biases in these features affect simulated water resources, extremes, and the atmospheric moisture and energy budgets. Diagnostics of AR objects, IVT distributions, vertical moisture-flux profiles, and column moisture budgets therefore provide a process-level check on EAMxx that global-mean precipitation or storm-track metrics alone do not.
